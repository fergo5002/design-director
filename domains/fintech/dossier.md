# Fintech and lending

## Who it is for

Two audiences who share one worry: what will this cost me, and can I trust it with my money.

- **Business owners and finance leads** (Mercury, Ramp, Brex, Tide, Wayflyer, Funding Circle, OnDeck, Shopify Capital). A founder opening a first account, a controller moving spend off a legacy bank, a shop owner who needs stock money before a busy season. They compare against their high-street bank, a loan broker, and whatever their accountant already uses. For lending they worry about the true cost, a hit to their credit score, a personal guarantee, and how fast the money actually lands. Every lender I looked at answers the credit score worry somewhere on the home page, and Funding Circle, OnDeck and Shopify Capital do it in the first screen ("without affecting your credit score", "No impact to your credit", "No credit checks, no impact").
- **Consumers** (Monzo, Starling, Wise, Wealthfront, Robinhood, Apple Card). They compare rates on a comparison site and fees against their old bank, and they look for proof the money is protected (FSCS, FDIC, a named partner bank) before they read anything else.

Both groups skim until the moment of commitment and then read every word. They arrive sceptical, because the category has taught them the catch is in the footnotes. At the top end (C. Hoare & Co.) the customer is buying a relationship and continuity, and the site does not sell at all.

## What the best do

- **Put the product's real numbers in the first screen.** Wise's hero is a working send calculator: "1,000.00 EUR" in, "105,697.12 INR" out, "Total fees 6.69 EUR, included in EUR amount", "Arrives Today - in 2 minutes", "Guaranteed for 52h". Wealthfront makes the rate the headline ("4.45% APY" at about 96px). Wayflyer puts the terms in one line under the headline: "From €10k to €20m. No personal guarantees. No equity taken."
- **Show a worked cost example, in money not percentages.** Wayflyer: "€100,000 financing, €7,200 fee, €107,200 to repay" and "You know the full cost before you accept." Wise shows the fee as one figure in the currency you pay. The weak lenders (National Funding, Biz2Credit, Shopify Capital) give ranges and speed but no cost at all.
- **Use a live counter as proof, and make it specific.** Stripe "Global GDP running on Stripe: 1.74380037%", Ramp "US corporate payments processed by Ramp: 0.9672259%" plus a ticker of agent counts, Wayflyer "Capital deployed so far: €6,446,167,800". All three set the figure in tabular or monospaced digits at 12 to 14px above a large headline.
- **Put the regulatory line beside the action, not only in the footer.** Mercury's "Mercury is a fintech company, not an FDIC-insured bank. Banking services provided through Choice Financial Group and Column N.A., Members FDIC" is in the first screen. Tide sets "FSCS-Protected Bank Account by ClearBank" in 12px directly under "Open an account". Wealthfront's dated rate footnote ("as of 10/01/2026") sits right under the buttons.
- **Treat figures as typography.** Wise sets amounts in its heavy condensed face at about 44px; Starling sets the whole interface (nav, buttons, labels) in CoFo Sans Semi-Mono against Avantt headings; Ramp puts each counter in its own monospace chip.
- **Join the email field to the primary button** (Mercury, Ramp, Brex, Wayflyer): one control, one decision, with a secondary "Launch demo" or "See a demo" beside it.
- **Spend boldness in one place.** Ramp's only colour is acid yellow #e4f222 on two buttons; Brex's is #ff3d00; Hoare's is a small gold label and arrow on bottle green.
- **Let a quantity drive the ornament.** Norges Bank's notes (Snøhetta) get coarser and choppier as the value rises, mapped to the Beaufort scale, and each denomination owns one colour.

## Conventions to keep

- **Regulated rate formats, in full.** "Representative 64.2% APR (variable), includes monthly fee" (Monzo), "4.00% AER/ 3.92% gross (variable) interest up to £25k" (Starling), "Base APY... as of 10/01/2026" (Wealthfront). People and regulators rely on the exact form; set it well rather than hiding it.
- **Protection named, with the partner and the limit.** "Eligible deposits are protected up to £120,000 by the FSCS" (Starling), "$5M FDIC insurance through our partner banks" (Mercury), "Tide is not a bank" (Tide), "All loans through Shopify Capital are issued by WebBank".
- **Credit score reassurance before the apply button** (Funding Circle, OnDeck, Shopify Capital, Wayflyer's "soft credit check").
- **Eligibility in plain numbers.** OnDeck: "1 Year in business, $100K business annual revenue, 625 personal FICO score". Wayflyer: "at least 6 months of trading history, monthly sales of €10,000 or more".
- **Decision and payout times as figures** ("decision in as little as 5 minutes", "Funds paid out typically within 48 hours", Funding Circle).
- **Third-party proof with a count**: "4.6 stars from 500+ reviews" (Wayflyer), "Excellent, 39,918 reviews on Trustpilot" (Tide), "Which? Recommended Provider" (Monzo, Starling).
- **Log in always visible** in the header; existing customers come to the home page to sign in.

## Conventions to break

- **Speed as the headline with cost in a footnote.** OnDeck leads with "Get funds as soon as 24 hours later" and keeps "The average rate for term loans is 53.2% APR" in the small print. Replace with Wayflyer's worked example: amount, fee, total to repay, in that order.
- **Ranges instead of rates.** "$25K-$2M+" with "reasonable rates and terms" (Biz2Credit) tells the borrower nothing. Show a representative example or a "from" rate per product, as Funding Circle does ("From 6.9% per year").
- **Unsourced counters.** Ramp's "3.2x faster than the average American business" and Pipe's "61% Average conversion rate" are numbers dressed as data. If a counter goes in, it needs a period and a method, or Pipe's footnote approach.
- **The stock smile with a card.** Funding Circle's hero (a smiling woman holding the card), OnDeck's bakery couple in aprons, Tide's sponsored athlete. Replace with the actual product doing something (Wise's calculator, Starling's real Spaces screen with "For Chester £50.00") or a named customer in their own place (National Funding's "Armando, Tool Repair & Construction, Dallas, TX" is the right instinct in the wrong styling).
- **Promotion as hero.** "2% cashback on all business spend" (Funding Circle) and announcement bars about funding rounds (Ramp, Brex, Wayflyer) push the actual product below the fold.
- **The press logo strip** (Lemonade: Fortune, WSJ, FT...) is a 2016 startup habit; a count of real customers or a named one does more.

## Category slop

What generated and template fintech looks like, from the three Framer demos (Payora, Finetech, FintechX) and the dated lenders (National Funding, OnDeck, Biz2Credit):

- **Layout.** Centred hero, an eyebrow pill above it ("Seamless Banking", "Track-Save-Send"), two pill buttons with circled arrows, a stack of five avatar faces with "Trusted by 90K Businesses" or "+25K", then a floating dashboard mock. Bento of feature cards: "Instant Payments, Smart Analytics, Secure Infrastructure, Global Transfers". A "three simple steps" row. A pricing table with Starter, Growth, Enterprise.
- **The dashboard mock.** Dark UI, "Good evening, Alex" with a waving-hand emoji, "Total Portfolio Value $58,420.00" (the same figure appears in two different templates), a donut chart and a wavy area chart, green "+12.4%" chips. No real product would greet you like that and no one can read it at that size.
- **Headlines.** Stripe's sentence with the nouns removed ("Financial Infrastructure for Modern Business"), three-beat imperatives ("Save it. Send it. Track it.", Shopify's own "Fund your business. Drive growth. Scale faster."), a glossy 3D "AI" tile dropped between two words, and the direct-lender shout in condensed caps with an exclamation mark ("Most lenders see a risk. We see a business owner!").
- **Numbers.** Plenty of them and none checkable: "60+ Businesses Served" two sections after "90K Businesses", "40% Platform Uptime", "98% Satisfaction", "+32% Faster" with no baseline, two different lifetime totals on one page (Biz2Credit). Ranges ("$6,000 to $500,000*") in place of any rate or cost.
- **Imagery.** Sky with clouds and green hills, a floating phone with a glossy gradient card ("Sydney Sweedy, $12,340.00"), a stock woman laughing at her phone, blob or half-circle masks around a smiling couple, orange diagonal slabs over a workshop photo.
- **Trust theatre.** A badge row (Trustpilot, Google, BBB A+, Inc. 5000), "Bank-level security" with a shield icon, testimonials from "Sarah Johnson, Manager" with no company, a "Trusted by thousands" section with no testimonials in it.
- **Copy.** "Seamless", "smarter", "effortless", "move money faster, safer, and smarter", "Your financial freedom starts here", "support@yourbrand.com" left in.

The quick test: if the page could sell a payments API, a budgeting app and an investing tool by changing one noun, it is slop. The best pages here (Wise, Wayflyer, Starling) could only be themselves because their first screen is their own numbers.

## Language

- **Type.** Two routes earn their place. A precise grotesque for the interface with figures that are designed, not defaulted: tabular or monospaced digits for amounts and counters (Ramp's chips, Wayflyer's "€6,446,167,800", Starling's semi-mono UI). Or a serif for trust and long horizons: Robinhood's Martina Plantijn at about 96px, Wealthfront's GT Alpina italic beside a bold rate, Hoare's caps serif, Stripe Press's Ivar. Inter for everything with one accent (Brex, the templates) is the category default and fails the swap test. Rates, APRs and fees need a size and weight of their own, never footnote grey only.
- **Colour.** Mostly one ground and one accent, held hard: Ramp white with #e4f222, Brex white with #ff3d00, Hoare bottle green with gold, Tide navy #060b4d, Wayflyer off-white #f9f9f8 with #1430ff. Banknotes show a better source: colour that means something (one colour per denomination, per product, per risk band). Purple-to-blue fields (Wealthfront, the templates) and gradient announcement bars are the generic choice.
- **Imagery.** The product doing its job beats any photo: the calculator, the real app screen in a hand, the card as an object (Brex, Apple Card). When people appear they should be named and in their own place, shot like documentary (Pipe's grainy film photo, Hoare's real room). Rendered dreamscapes (Mercury's desk on a misty hillside) sit close to generated imagery and should not be copied.
- **Voice.** Specific and calm. The best copy is a sentence of terms: "No personal guarantees. No equity taken." (Wayflyer), "You know the full cost before you accept." Jokes work for consumer brands with years of trust (Monzo's "not the crunchy, tasty kind") and read as flippant on a lender. Never "seamless" or "unlock".
- **Motion.** Numbers that tick (Stripe, Ramp, Wayflyer counters) are the one motion that belongs to money, and only when the figure is real and explained. Everything else should be still and quick; a borrower waiting on a fade-in is a borrower getting suspicious.

## Open questions

- **Journeys not walked.** I looked at home pages only (fold screenshots at desktop and phone, plus the page text). I did not walk an application, quote, eligibility or calculator flow on any site, so how the best handle KYC, bank connection, offer screens and declines is unknown. Walking Wayflyer's eligibility steps, Funding Circle's product selector and Wise's calculator as far as possible without submitting would answer it.
- **Below the fold.** Screenshots are first-screen only; the lower sections are known from page text, not pixels. A full-page render of Wayflyer, Wise and Mercury would show how pricing and FAQ sections are laid out.
- **Blocked.** Revolut served a Cloudflare check to both the renderer and WebFetch, and Lendio returned 403, so neither is in the library. Biz2Credit blocked the renderer (CloudFront 403) and is recorded from text only.
- **Credit unions and community lenders.** No credit union or community bank was looked at; the leader reference for that end of the market is a private bank. A handful of well-regarded credit union sites would fill it.
- **US versus UK and EU.** Robinhood's US URL served the EU site from here, so the US investing disclosures (FINRA, SIPC) were not seen. Regulated wording differs by market, and a brief should say which market it is for.
- **Dark-mode and app surfaces.** Only marketing sites were studied, not logged-in products or statements.
