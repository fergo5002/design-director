# Independent stays

## Who it is for

Two people use these sites and the design has to serve both.

The guest is usually planning a short break for two, a family or a group of friends, often with a dog. They have nearly always found the place somewhere else first (Airbnb, Booking.com, a curator like Kip Hideaways or Canopy & Stars, a newspaper list) and come to the owner's own site to check two things: is it cheaper or more flexible here, and is it real. Which? found the same cottage on the same dates varying by more than £600 between sites (prices checked March 2021), and warned that booking direct "can leave you vulnerable to fraud", advising guests to confirm the property exists, read third-party reviews and pay by credit card. So the direct site is judged against a platform listing that already shows a price, a calendar, reviews and a cancellation policy on one screen. Their specific worries, from what the best sites answer up front: the total for their dates, the minimum stay and changeover day (Irish Landmark Trust: 2 nights minimum, "1-night stays are not permitted"; Inis Meáin: Wednesday to Sunday only), whether dogs are allowed and how many (Unique Homestays makes it a guest type: one, two or three dogs), the deposit (Canopy & Stars: "Book with a 25% deposit"), and what is within walking distance (Kip: "Walk to a pub", "Walk to a shop", "15 minutes down to the golden sand").

The owner is a family or a small team who also clean, cook and answer the phone. The site must be cheap to keep true: one price table, one calendar fed by the booking engine, photographs that do not date. The guest's testimonial on a Donegal owner portal says it plainly: they liked it because of "the price details given immediately".

## What the best do

They put the stay's terms where the decision is made, in plain type. Inis Meáin sets its rates as a typeset list, one line per house per season, for one fixed stay ("Currach House, Jun, Jul, Aug, €3,750", 5 day/4 night Wednesday to Sunday, "Rates include cleaning, fees and taxes", and what is not included: the ferry and taxis). Irish Landmark Trust shows "From £346 for 2 nights" as one of four icon facts directly under the property name (with "Sleeps 2", "2 Dogs Allowed", "Wood Burning Stove"). Kip shows "From £175/night" under the name and "(sleeps 5)" in the one-line summary. Canopy & Stars cards read county in small caps, name in a serif, "from £128 p/n", "Book now".

They show availability as a picture, not a form. Irish Landmark Trust's calendar marks every bookable arrival day as a filled dark green circle and booked spans as grey bars with slanted ends for changeovers, with the instruction "Click arrival date for cost of stay" above it; clicking a day opens a short "Your booking" panel (property, "Arrival: Mon 16 November 2026", a "Stay for" select of 2 to 13 nights, adults, children, infants, one "BOOK NOW"). Living Architecture draws two months side by side in the same 1px ruled grid as the rest of the site and underlines in red only the days a stay may start (Mondays and Fridays), with one sentence of instruction: "Click on an underlined red available start date. Once selected, click on an underlined red available end date." Inis Meáin draws a strip chart, one row per house, one square per bookable night (Wednesday to Saturday) across the month, so the two houses can be compared at a glance, and offers an "Availability Notification" for cancellations instead of a dead end. Kip strikes through unavailable dates on grey and underlines available ones in green inside a sticky card titled "Book this hideaway." with "Who's coming?" above the calendar; once two dates are picked the calendar collapses into a five-line summary ("Number of nights: 4", "From: Mon, 16 Nov 2026", "To: Fri, 20 Nov 2026", "Number of guests: 1", "Price: £645") with one "Book now" under it and the Trustpilot score (4.8, 468 reviews) under that. That summary is the moment of decision, and it is plain text, not a widget.

They keep one booking action on every screen, phone included. Coombeshead Farm runs a full-width slate grey band above the logo that just says "Book a Room". Ballymaloe House pins a two-part bar to the bottom of the phone screen: a phone icon and "Book Now". Fogo Island Inn keeps a navy "Reserve" in the phone header beside the menu.

They draw the place, and photograph it in use. Coombeshead's engraved tree clump, Ballymaloe's engraved portrait of the house as a seal, Inis Meáin's line map of the island's field walls in the footer, Wildland's woodcut roundel. Photography is daylight and weather on real buildings (Coombeshead's slate roof half hidden by hedgerow, Sawday's Skye inn captioned "The Dunvegan, Isle of Skye") and people or dogs using the place (fforest's campfire and spaniel, Ballymaloe's guests crossing the gravel with their bags).

They write like a person who lives there. Ballyvolane names the owners in its second paragraph and Ballymaloe says the house "was once our family home"; Kip prints the owners' own words and then three short lines: "She loves", "We love", "You may not love" (the last one honest: "The track in is a bit bumpy... Sorry, no pets"). Inis Meáin prints the day's sunrise, sunset and tide times in the footer. Fogo Island Inn turns a value into a number ("100% of operating surpluses are reinvested into the community").

## Conventions to keep

- A price near the name, in the form the stay is sold: per night ("From £175/night", "from £128 p/n") for flexible lets, per stay with the minimum written in ("From £346 for 2 nights") when there is a minimum. Say what the price includes.
- A real calendar on the property page, with booked dates visibly different from free ones and the changeover rule shown on it, not buried in terms (Irish Landmark Trust, Kip, Inis Meáin).
- Sleeps, bedrooms, dogs and one or two signature facts as a short row of icons or words directly under the name.
- A phone number and the owner's name somewhere obvious. Unique Homestays sets the number above its nav; Ballymaloe makes calling half of the phone booking bar.
- One persistent book action, labelled for what it does ("Book a Room", "Reserve", "Check Availability"), never two competing buttons of equal weight.
- Once dates are chosen, a plain summary of nights, dates, guests and total before the book button (Kip). Go one better than Kip and break the total down: nights, cleaning, dog fee (Irish Landmark Trust charges "£30 per pet"), deposit due now.
- Booking conditions, deposit and cancellation one tap from the calendar (Irish Landmark Trust's "Read Full Booking Conditions" sits beside its FAQ tabs).
- What is near, with walking times (Kip's feature icons; Cicerone's route row of time, distance, ascent and descent is the better model).

## Conventions to break

- Hiding price until the guest has typed dates. Unique Homestays, Fogo Island Inn, Ballymaloe and Ballyvolane show no figure on the home page at all, while the platform listing the guest just left did. Replace it with a "from" figure on every card and the full season table one tap away.
- A booking engine that looks like someone else's site. Irish Landmark Trust (Supercontrol) and Living Architecture both drop the calendar in as an iframe with its own type and buttons, and Irish Landmark Trust's promise "Click arrival date for cost of stay" is broken on the first click, which asks for nights and guests before any figure. Style the engine to the site, and show a price the moment a date is touched.
- The full-screen hero carousel with dots and arrows (Gregans Castle, Hidden Ireland, Living Architecture, de Blacam and Meagher). One strong photograph or one slow video does more; Inis Meáin and Fogo use a single moving shot.
- Interrupting the first screen. Newsletter modals on load (Irish Landmark Trust, fforest) and consent walls that fill the phone fold (Gregans Castle's Cookiebot panel, Unique Homestays) mean the first thing a guest sees is a form. Use a small, quiet consent bar and no modal.
- Navigation that lists every income line (Ballyvolane has nine items plus six icons; weddings, retreats, vouchers and news crowd out the stay). Lead with the houses and booking; put the rest in the footer.
- The tabbed property page (Property Details, Facilities, Tariff, Availability, Gallery, Location on the Donegal portal). Make it one scrolling page with the booking card fixed beside it, as Kip does.
- Slogan taglines that say nothing ("Everything you desire, nothing you expect", "A Truly Magical House"). Say where it is, what it is built of and how many it sleeps.

## Category slop

There are two kinds, and both are easy to make by accident.

The dated kind is the owner portal and the early 2010s template: script or handwriting faces for headings and tabs (Donegal Cottage Holidays' folder tabs, Hidden Ireland's script tagline "Stay In Classic, Luxury & Stylish Country Houses"), several loud button colours fighting (Hidden Ireland's rose, green and a red bevelled "CHECK AVAILABILITY!" with an exclamation mark), clip-art feature badges (sea views, free wifi, open fireplace), purple or teal bands, a free-text "Search here..." box, a carousel with arrows, and generic spa copy ("your blood pressure will go down just walking through the door", "Experience the Peaceful and Serene Spaces"). Price and availability live behind tabs, and the layout is a fixed-width page shrunk on a phone.

The generated kind is what Wix and AI builders such as Zarla produce now, and it has learnt to fake specificity. The headline is a formula, "[Adjective] [Type] in [Place]", with the place set in a mustard or tan accent ("Secluded Cabin Rentals in Gatlinburg", "Small Guest House in Portland, ME"). Above it sits a tracked capital eyebrow of dot-separated facts between two short rules ("FORTY ACRES · TWELVE MINUTES TO THE PARK GATE"); below it a filled "Check Dates" or "Ask About Dates" next to an outlined "See The Cabins", then three pill chips with tick icons ("Five Cabins Only", "Dogs Welcome", "Wood Included"). The phone number in the header is a 555 number and the header repeats the same button. The copy sounds lived-in ("nobody you can see from your porch") but there is still no price, no calendar and no changeover day above the fold. Wix's B&B template is the older, emptier version: "Stylish Urban Retreat / Modern comfort in the heart of the city", a navy pill "Explore", then two stock still-lifes of a palm frond and a vase in window light.

The tells to check for: an eyebrow of three facts, the place name in an accent colour, tick-icon pills, two hero buttons, a stock interior with no building in it, any price written as "Ask about dates", and a calendar that only appears after a click.

## Language

The source is the building and its ground: stone, slate, lime and whitewash, field walls, the river or the coast, and the owners' own documents (a rates card, a visitors' book, the house rules). Every strong reference draws from one of these, and the weak ones from a template.

Type: one serif with some age in it, used with discipline, does more than a pairing. Coombeshead sets everything in Franklin Caslon and Georgia Pro; St. JOHN uses Georgia alone, place names in wide bold capitals and the address in italic; Unique Homestays uses EB Garamond capitals tracked wide for the wordmark with Brandon Grotesque for the nav; Fogo pairs Metrisch and Franie. Small caps or widely spaced capitals for place and house names (Little Toller's covers, de Blacam and Meagher's letterspaced name) read as a printed guide. Avoid Roboto, Open Sans and Outfit as the voice: they carry 84% of the text on Canopy & Stars and all of the Zarla templates.

Colour: take it from the materials and keep it to one family. Warm cream or limewash grounds (Coombeshead, Fogo, St. JOHN's white), a slate or deep green for type and the book action (Coombeshead's slate band, Irish Landmark Trust's dark green calendar circles, Canopy's #324f45 footer), and at most one earthy accent like Little Toller's deep red. Not mustard, not the builder blue, not purple.

Imagery: one honest photograph per screen, of the building in its weather or of people and dogs using it, captioned with the place (Sawday's). A drawn mark from the place (engraved house, tree, field-wall map) carries identity better than a logo. Avoid styled empty interiors that could be anywhere.

Voice: name the owners, give numbers and walking times, state the rules plainly the way St. JOHN states that "Menus will not go online until 11.00am", and say what a guest may not love. Inis Meáin's English over Irish headings ("Rates / Rátaí") show how a second language can be structure, not decoration.

Motion: almost none. The best move once, slowly, in the hero (Inis Meáin's aerial coast with the island outline drawn over it, Fogo's wind in long grass) and are still everywhere else. A booking card that stays put while the page scrolls (Kip) is the only motion that earns its keep.

## Open questions

- What does checkout look like after dates are chosen? I saw calendars and price summaries but no deposit, payment or cancellation screens, because I did not go past date selection. Walking two booking engines to the payment page (without paying) would answer it.
- The Landmark Trust (UK), The Pig, Lodgify's template demo and Simply Owners all returned Cloudflare or bot blocks to the renderer, so the UK's best-known historic-building let and a leading country-house hotel group are missing. A person browsing them would close that gap.
- No conversion evidence: I do not know whether showing price first actually raises direct bookings for small owners, only that the best references do it and one guest praised it. Booking-engine benchmarks or an owner's before and after numbers would answer it.
- Phone share of traffic and booking for small cottages is unknown; every reference was judged at both widths, but which one dominates for this category is a guess.
- How guests check a direct site is genuine (reviews widgets, payment logos, registration numbers such as Fáilte Ireland's) was seen only in passing; it needs its own look.
