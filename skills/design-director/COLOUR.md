# Colour

## Banned combinations

- Purple to blue gradient on white. The single most AI-looking thing on the internet.
- `#F4F1EA` cream with a terracotta accent and a serif display.
- Near-black with one acid green or one vermilion pop, if that is the whole idea.
- Three brand colours sprayed evenly across everything.
- Any palette generated by picking a hue and taking its 50 to 900 Tailwind ramp unmodified.

## The two systems that actually work

Every specimen in the library does one of these. Pick one, commit to it.

### A. One accent, everything else neutral

**Attio** is the reference. A cool grey ramp (`#eeeff1` `#e6e7ea` `#cdced2` `#75777c` `#242629` `#101112`) and exactly one hue, `#266df0`. The CTA is impossible to miss because nothing else competes.

Use when the page has one job. Funnels, signup, product marketing, dashboards.

Rules:
- The accent appears on the primary action and nowhere else that is not a link.
- Build the neutral ramp with a **slight hue bias toward the accent**. A pure `#808080` grey reads as unconsidered. Attio's greys lean cool because the accent is blue.
- Semantic colour (success, warning, danger) is a **separate system** and does not count as your accent. Linear keeps `--diff-new-color:#27A644` and `--diff-old-color:#F34E52` apart from brand.

### B. A world per item

**Stripe Press** is the reference, and it is the best colour system in the library. Every book gets its own ground and gradient. Verified values:

```
#143199  deep ultramarine     #4D1A28  oxblood        #96DCED  sky
#201E8E  indigo               #93935F  olive          #FFB55E  amber
#2328A0  cobalt               #C1B676  sand           #0AEB9A  mint
#0d121f  midnight             #6E665B  warm taupe     #ffd943  signature yellow
#1D1A15  bistre               #442C25  bitter chocolate

linear-gradient(#ff815a, #ffaf5b, #ffdf5b)
linear-gradient(#e7d4e5, #cad9e5, #eeebcc, #e9d…)
```

These are saturated and confident, not tints of one brand hue, and almost none are on an AI shortlist. Use when you have a **set** of things: packages, case studies, collections, chapters, plans. Give each its own world instead of flattening one palette across all of them.

## Building a palette from nothing

**Do not pick colours. Build them.** Every step below is executable with
`tools/palette.py`, because "bias the neutrals" and "check both grounds" are not
things anyone can do by eye, and a checklist you cannot execute is a wish.

1. **Start from the subject's own world**, never from a colour wheel or a mood board
   of other websites. Go to the actual objects, instruments and conventions of the
   thing. A sauna is heat, cedar, steam, cold plunge, low winter light. A barrister
   is oak, ink, paper, brass. **The Irish sea is not blue**: it is grey-green water,
   bladderwrack, granite, and the one genuinely saturated colour in the whole
   business is the magenta that Admiralty charts use for navigational information.
   That last one is a real convention with a reason, which is exactly why no model
   reaches for it and exactly why it reads as chosen.
2. **Pick the ground first.** Ground decides the mood. Everything else reacts to it.
3. **Build ramps in OKLCH, not HSL.** Even steps in OKLCH look even; even steps in
   HSL do not, which is why hand-built ramps always have a dead patch in the middle.

   ```bash
   python tools/palette.py ramp --hue 172:148 --chroma 0.022:0.050 \
                                --light 0.30:0.87 --steps 7 --css-var sea
   ```

   Drifting the hue slightly along the ramp (`172:148`) is what stops it looking
   like one colour with the lights turned down.
4. **Bias the neutrals toward the accent.** A pure `#808080` reads as unconsidered.
   A near-black at `H 140, C 0.016` is still black to the eye and is not grey.
5. **One accent.** If you need a second, make it analogous or drop its saturation.
6. **Prove it, per theme.** Running both themes in one pass produces cross-theme
   noise (light ink on a dark ground was never going to pass). Run them separately:

   ```bash
   python tools/palette.py check --strict \
     --ground "page=#ebe7d9,sheet=#f8f5ec" --fg "ink=#0e130d,accent=#a0005b"
   ```

## Contrast, non-negotiable

- Body text **4.5:1**. Large text (24px+, or 19px+ bold) and UI edges **3:1**.
  Measured. `--strict` exits 1 so it can gate a build.
- **Test the accent as text, not only as a fill.** `#ff3f0a` on `#ecebe7` is about
  2.95:1 and fails; on near-black it passes. An accent that fails as text becomes a
  fill with dark text on it, or a rule. That is what Alaris does.
- **Measure the composite, not the tokens.** This is the one people miss. A wash, a
  scrim, a gradient overlay or a tinted panel sits between the text and its ground,
  and the token pair proves nothing about it:

  ```bash
  python tools/palette.py blend --over "sheet=#1d2c26" --tint "#b3d2b9" \
                                --alpha 0.5 --fg "ink=#ded8c6,muted=#a1bfa8"
  ```

  A 50% tint that looked harmless once took a caption from 7.3:1 to **2.17:1**. At
  14% the same design passes everywhere and reads almost identically. If the wash
  has to be strong, move it off the text: a solid key bar down the edge plus a
  faint tint gives you the same signal with none of the cost.

- **Text over a colour scale must use pure `#000` and `#fff`.** Choosing the better
  of two inks leaves a dead zone unless the pair is at the extremes. With `#0e130d`
  and `#f8f5ec` the best available contrast bottoms out at **4.15:1** in the
  mid-range and fails. With pure black and white the two contrast curves cross at
  **4.58:1**, so every possible background clears 4.5. Provable, and worth knowing:
  the softer, more tasteful ink pair is the one that fails.
- Never encode meaning in hue alone. Pair with an icon, a label, a weight, a
  position, or make the scale vary in lightness so it survives colour blindness and
  a black-and-white printer.
- Placeholder grey on grey fails almost every time. Check it.

## Dark mode

Do it at token level or not at all.

```css
:root{ --ground:#ecebe7; --ink:#17191b; --accent:#ff3f0a }
@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark){
  :root{ --ground:#141618; --ink:#e8e7e3 }
}
:root[data-theme="dark"]{ /* same overrides, must beat the media query */ }
:root[data-theme="light"]{ /* and back again */ }
```

Style components through the tokens, never inside the media query. Rules:

- **Do not invert.** A dark theme is designed, not computed. Aesop's cream does not become charcoal by flipping a value.
- Pure `#000` grounds are harsh. Linear uses `#08090a`, Alaris `#0a0a0a`. Sit just off black.
- Reduce accent saturation slightly on dark grounds or it vibrates.
- Elevation in dark mode is lighter surfaces, not bigger shadows.
- A design that deliberately commits to one world (Aesop, Lando) may stay single-theme. Make it a decision, not an omission.

## Glow and shadow

- Real glows sit at **2 to 3% opacity**. Linear's tokens are literally `--glow-opacity: 0.02` and `0.03`. Yours is probably ten times too strong.
- Layer three soft shadows rather than one hard one.
- For an organic bloom, stack offset radial gradients and blur the whole layer heavily (Alaris blurs a four-gradient stack by 42px). No image needed.
- Coloured shadows tinted toward the accent read as considered. Pure black shadows read as default.
